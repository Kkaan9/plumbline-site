What we make

Asset Audit

Coming to the Unity Asset Store

Unity projects collect assets nothing points at any more: textures from a pack imported for one prop, audio that got replaced, models from an experiment. They ship with the build and slow down every import. Asset Audit finds them.

It will not call an asset dead unless it can prove it. Unity can load an asset from a string — Resources.Load, Shader.Find, an addressable key — leaving no trail to follow. A tool that ignores that reports those assets as unused, and people delete live content. So there are three answers here, not two.

  • In use
    Something reaches it. Cannot be selected for removal.
  • Needs review
    It might be dead, but that cannot be proven. Your call, with the reason shown.
  • Unused
    Nothing in the project reaches it. Safe to remove.

How we build

  1. 01

    Prove it, or say you cannot

    An honest maybe is worth more than a confident wrong answer. Where the evidence runs out, the tool says so and hands the decision back, rather than rounding uncertainty down to a delete button.

  2. 02

    Every answer carries its reason

    A verdict without a reason asks for faith. Each finding states the rule that produced it, so you can disagree with the tool on the evidence instead of on trust.

  3. 03

    One irreversible step, clearly marked

    Removing assets moves them aside with a manifest, so undoing is a move back rather than a recovery. Exactly one action deletes anything for good, and it says so before you take it.
